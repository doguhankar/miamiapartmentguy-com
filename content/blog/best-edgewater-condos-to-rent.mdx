# Best Edgewater Condos to Rent: Buildings vs. Managed Communities

**Edgewater has two rental games: owned condo buildings (strict, bureaucratic, expensive) and managed rental communities (flexible, pricier, less hassle). Here's how to pick.**

Edgewater is Miami's compact neighborhood—walkable, close to downtown, newer construction dominating. But there's a split between where you live here: traditional condo buildings (where owners rent units) and managed rental communities (where a company owns and rents everything). The difference shapes your entire lease.

## Condo Buildings: Strict, Slower, Cheaper on Paper

These are towers where owners buy units and lease them out individually. You're renting from an individual owner whose unit is part of an HOA. This means: owner approval (they see your credit, income, background), HOA restrictions (no dogs over 25 lbs, no smoking, quiet hours 10 PM–7 AM), and lease limits (many HOAs cap how many units can be rented—yours might not be available next year).

Approval takes 2–4 weeks. Your landlord is someone managing their investment, not a company. Good news: rent is usually $100–300 lower than managed communities. Bad news: lease flexibility is lower, and if the owner sells, you might get a non-renewal notice at lease end.

Typical condo rental: $2,000–$3,500 for a one-bedroom, depending on floor and view. You'll pay first month, last month, deposit, and sometimes an HOA approval fee ($250–$500).

## Managed Rental Communities: Flexible, Professional, Pricier

These are buildings owned by a management company (not individual owners). Leasing is instant—no owner approval, just the company's credit and background check. Lease terms are flexible: 6-month, 12-month, even 18-month options. They welcome corporate tenants and are usually dog-friendly (with a pet deposit).

Amenities are higher-end: rooftop pools, gyms, concierge, package rooms, dedicated parking. Move-in is faster—you might sign and move in 10 days instead of 30.

Rent is higher: $2,200–$3,800 for a one-bedroom, but it includes amenity value. Approval is straightforward—you pass their underwriting or you don't. No owner discretion.

## Which One to Pick

Condo buildings are cheaper and have more personality—you're part of a smaller community. But you're waiting for an owner to approve you, and lease renewal is uncertain. Go condo if you're stable, your credit is solid, and you want the lowest rent.

Managed communities are easier, faster, and more reliable long-term. You're paying for convenience and professionalism. Go managed if you're new to Miami, you're corporate, you want flexibility, or you have a dog.
