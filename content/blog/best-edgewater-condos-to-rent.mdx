Edgewater has a mix of individually owned condos and professionally managed buildings available for rent, which makes this comparison worth running here specifically — though the underlying questions apply to any Miami neighborhood with a similar mix; see our [condo vs. apartment guide](/blog/condo-vs-apartment-rental-miami) for the general version. Ask your agent or the listing directly whether a specific unit is individually owned or managed before assuming either way. An individually owned condo and a managed apartment can both be good rental options. The difference is who makes the decisions, who handles the unit and what approvals you need. Neither format automatically means cheaper rent, faster approval or a guaranteed renewal.

## Identify every decision-maker

For an individually owned condo, ask who represents the owner and whether the association has a separate approval process. For a managed apartment, confirm the leasing entity and management contact. Do not assume one company owns every apartment in a building based only on how the lobby looks. Know who signs the lease and who receives each payment.

## Get the approval sequence in writing

Ask what documents are required, which applications are separate and when a complete application can be reviewed. Ask about any interviews, move reservations or other prerequisites. Timelines depend on the property and application; this guide does not promise instant leasing or a standard two-to-four-week condo approval. Leave room between approval and your planned moving date.

## Compare deposits and fees line by line

Request all amounts charged by the owner, management and association, if applicable. Mark which are refundable, which are one-time and which recur. Confirm parking and access-device arrangements. Compare equivalent lease terms and costs rather than assuming condo rent is lower or managed rent includes a fixed premium. Use your move-in worksheet to track when cash is due.

## Clarify repairs and shared amenities

Ask who handles an appliance problem, an after-hours issue and a building-wide interruption. Get the reporting process and relevant contacts. Confirm whether any listed amenity is available to renters and whether reservation rules or charges apply. An attractive lobby does not tell you how quickly a unit repair will be handled.

## Discuss the end of the lease before the beginning

Ask how renewal discussions work and read the lease's notice and termination provisions. Do not treat renewal as guaranteed in either rental format. If you hear a claim about sale, renewal or early termination rights, check the actual agreement and obtain qualified advice for a legal question. Choose using the documented terms and the apartment's fit, not a stereotype about condos or management companies.
