Most Miami buildings advertise some mix of pool, gym, and a lounge or workspace, bundled into a monthly amenity fee you pay whether you use them or not — though exactly which amenities are included, and how well they're maintained, varies a lot by property. The fee itself isn't the problem; paying it for amenities you'll never touch is. The only way to know which is which is to check against your own habits, not the brochure.

## Ask what you'd actually use

Before touring, make a short honest list: which of these would you realistically use on a normal week — pool, gym, coworking space, concierge or package room? Be specific about frequency, not aspirational about it. A pool you'd use twice a summer carries a different value than one you'd use weekly.

## Visit at the hours you'd actually use it

A gym or coworking space that looks great empty during a midday tour can be a different experience at 7 a.m. or 6 p.m. Ask to see it, or ask residents informally, at the time you'd actually be there. Ask directly: "Can I reserve a desk in the coworking space, or is it first-come-first-served?" and "What are the pool and gym hours?" — a space with restrictive hours relative to your schedule is worth less to you regardless of how it looks.

## Some amenities depend entirely on your situation

A coworking space is genuinely valuable if you work remotely and your unit doesn't give you a quiet place to take calls — see our [working-from-home guide](/blog/miami-apartments-working-from-home) for how to evaluate that specifically. A package room matters a lot if you receive frequent deliveries and barely at all if you don't. Don't let a building's amenity list sell you on value you won't personally draw from it.

## Ask for the fee breakdown, not just the total

Request what the amenity fee actually covers and whether any portion is optional or creditable if you opt out of specific services. Not every building will itemize this, but asking surfaces how transparent the leasing office is being — and sometimes genuinely gets you a reduction.

## What to ask before signing

- "Which amenities do residents actually use most, based on what you see?"
- "Are the pool and gym hours compatible with a typical workday?"
- "Is the coworking space reservable, or often full?"
- "Does the amenity fee cover anything that's currently under renovation or changing soon?"

A high amenity fee isn't automatically a bad deal, and a low one isn't automatically a good one. It depends entirely on whether what's included matches how you'll actually live.
