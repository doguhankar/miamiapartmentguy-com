# Working From Home in a Miami Apartment: Desk Space, Internet, and the Noise Problem

**Remote work changes everything. You need dedicated desk space, reliable internet, quiet during calls, and a window to avoid losing your mind. Most Miami apartments don't optimize for this.**

You signed a lease two years ago when you went to the office. Now you're remote and your one-bedroom has no designated workspace, your internet drops when it rains, your neighbor's bass shakes your desk during conference calls, and you're slowly going insane staring at the same 400 square feet.

Remote work in Miami apartments needs specific setups. Here's what actually matters.

## Desk Space: Nook, Bedroom Corner, or Tragedy

**Good setup**: Separate room (second bedroom turned office) or a dedicated corner in the living room with a door you can close. $2,000–$2,500 for a one-bedroom with this flexibility.

**Okay setup**: A bedroom corner with a real desk, not a tiny table in the middle of your living room. You can close the bedroom door and have meetings. $1,800–$2,200 one-bedroom.

**Awful setup**: Your desk is in the living room. You're on a call, someone knocks on the door, delivery person rings, your roommate watches TV behind you. $1,400–$1,800 studios and tiny one-bedrooms. Avoid.

Some new buildings offer dedicated coworking spaces (included in amenities). Problem: 200 other residents want to use it during 9 AM–5 PM. It's crowded, loud, and a commute from your unit.

## Internet Reliability: More Important Than You Think

Miami gets humidity spikes and occasional storms. ISP lines are sometimes exposed. Your internet dropping for 20 minutes in a meeting is unacceptable.

Ask the building: "What internet providers serve this unit?" If it's only one provider, you have zero backup. If there are two (Cox + Fiber or similar), you can get both—expensive but redundancy matters for work.

Test the speed before signing. Download 100 Mbps, upload 20 Mbps is the baseline for video calls. Ask the leasing office if anyone's complained about drops.

Fiber > cable > DSL. That's the hierarchy.

## Quiet During Call Hours

Your neighbor's music, sirens, construction, Metromover rumbling—all of it is audible. 

Ask the building: "Which units are quietest for calls?" Corner units beat middle units. High floors beat low floors (less street noise). North-facing (away from clubs/bars) beats south-facing.

If you're in Wynwood or Brickell, accept that noise happens. Plan calls for 7–10 AM before the day starts, or find a coworking space for important presentations.

**Pro move**: Get noise-canceling headphones and a good microphone. The microphone suppresses room noise. Boom—your neighbor's TV is inaudible on calls.

## Window and Light

Working in an interior room with no window is depression waiting to happen. Make sure your unit has at least one room with a window. Bay views are a bonus. East-facing is best (morning light, cool afternoon). West-facing is brutal (afternoon heat on your screen).

## Amenity Workspace

Some new buildings (Cortland, Equity properties, newer boutique buildings) have included coworking spaces—Wifi, desks, conference rooms, zoom pods. This changes everything if your unit is small. Built-in backup workspace.

Check if it's included or if there's a fee ($50–$100/month sometimes).

## Real Setup Cost

If your apartment doesn't have dedicated office space:
- Desk: $300–$800
- Chair: $200–$600
- Monitor: $200–$400
- Internet (second provider): $60–$100/month

Total: $760–$1,900 up-front + $60–$100/month ongoing. That's $7,200–$13,100 over 18 months.

For that price, a two-bedroom (if available) at $200–$300/month premium is actually cheaper long-term.

## What to Ask Before Signing

- "How many units can I get a second internet provider?"
- "Which rooms have the quietest walls?"
- "Do you have included coworking space?"
- "What's the upload/download speed?"
- "Are there windows in all bedrooms?"
