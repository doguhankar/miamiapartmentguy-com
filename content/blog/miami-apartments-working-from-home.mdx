A unit that works fine for sleeping and weekends can fall apart once you're on video calls in it eight hours a day. If you're renting somewhere you'll also be working from, add a short list of work-specific checks to your tour before you sign.

## Confirm real desk space, not just floor plan square footage

A floor plan doesn't tell you whether there's a wall you can put a desk against with your back to a door, or whether your only option is a corner of the living room. Walk the actual unit and identify where you'd physically set up, including whether you can close a door during calls. If the building advertises a coworking space as a backup, ask about its hours and whether desks need to be reserved — a backup space you can't reliably get into on short notice isn't much of a backup.

## Ask about internet providers and what backup actually means

Ask which internet providers currently serve the unit. If only one wired provider serves the building, that doesn't automatically mean you have zero backup — a mobile hotspot or a cellular data plan can cover you during an outage — but it does mean less built-in redundancy than a unit with two wired options. Ask the leasing office if any residents have reported recurring drops, and if possible, ask to test the connection in the actual unit before signing, not just in the leasing office.

## Treat your call hours like a noise check

Your work hours create specific quiet-hour requirements, even if you don't mind ambient noise the rest of the day. Use the same process as any other noise check — see our [guide to checking apartment noise](/blog/check-apartment-noise-before-signing) — but focus it on your actual working hours rather than evenings. Ask specifically which side of the building faces nightlife, delivery areas, or Metromover if that applies.

## Light and floor matter, but verify rather than assume

A workspace with no natural light wears on you over a full workday in a way it might not on a weekend. Check which direction the room you'll work in faces. Floor level can affect light and street noise, but not in a fixed, predictable way across every building — see our [high floor vs. low floor guide](/blog/high-floor-vs-low-floor-miami) for what's actually worth verifying rather than assuming.

## What to ask before signing

- "How many wired internet providers currently serve this unit, and what's the backup plan if the connection drops?"
- "Is there a coworking or business space included, and what are its hours?"
- "Which side of the building is quietest during the day?"
- "Are there windows in the room I'd use as a workspace?"

None of this needs to be perfect. It needs to match how you actually work, which is a different question than how the unit looks in photos.
