# How to Tour Miami Apartments Remotely: Video Calls, Checklists, and Red Flags

**You're in New York. The apartment is in Miami. A 30-minute video tour doesn't cut it. Here's how to actually know what you're getting.**

Remote apartment hunting is brutal. You can't touch the walls, turn on the HVAC, check the hot water, or listen for neighbor noise. Video tours feel incomplete because they are. But you can get good information if you know what to ask and what to look for on video.

## Before the Video Tour: The Checklist

Have these questions written down and ready:

**Unit-specific:**
- "Can you show me the bedroom with the door closed? How much light is blocked?"
- "Turn on the HVAC. What does it sound like?"
- "Run the shower and sink. Is water pressure good?"
- "Open all windows. Can you hear outside noise?"
- "Show me the closet. How deep is it?"
- "Is there a dishwasher, washer/dryer, or hookups?"
- "What's the cell phone signal like in the bedroom?" (Actually test it.)
- "Show me the electrical outlets. How many per room?"

**Building-specific:**
- "Show me the lobby and common areas."
- "What do the hallways look like? Are they bright or dim?"
- "Show the parking garage (if applicable). How far is the walk to the elevators?"
- "What time is garbage pickup and where is it?"
- "Is there a package room? How is it organized?"
- "Show the gym/pool area."

**Neighborhood:**
- "Step outside the building and show me the surrounding block. What do I see?"
- "What's the foot traffic like right now? (It's [time of day], so this is typical.)"
- "Show me nearby restaurants/shops from the building entrance."

**The Honesty Test:**
- "What do current residents complain about most?"
- "What's the average length of residency?" (High turnover = problems.)
- "What's the application approval rate?" (If it's <70%, there's something people don't like.)

## Red Flags on Video

- **Dim hallways**: Old building, cheap lighting, not maintained.
- **Thin walls**: You can see through gaps or light comes through. Sound isolation is bad.
- **HVAC sounds loud**: Aging system, potential for breakdowns.
- **Weak water pressure**: Plumbing issues, city water problem, or building issue.
- **Cell signal is zero**: Building materials block signal. Your work calls will drop.
- **Leasing agent is evasive**: Won't answer direct questions. Red flag.
- **Video quality is potato**: They're hiding something, or they don't care. Either way, bad sign.
- **Neighbor noise is audible on video**: It's actually worse in person.
- **Parking garage is poorly lit or empty**: Either management is failing or the building is emptying out.

## What a Good Remote Tour Looks Like

- Leasing agent is responsive and patient.
- They show you everything you ask (not just the "best" angles).
- They answer hard questions directly.
- Unit sounds good (quiet), looks well-maintained, has decent light.
- Hallways are bright and clean.
- Building entrance is appealing (not beat-to-hell).
- They offer multiple video tour times (so you can see different times of day).

## After the Video Tour: Verification Steps

**Send a follow-up email** asking for:
- Floor plan (PDF)
- List of all fees (parking, amenity, pet, etc.)
- Current lease terms (lease length options, renewal process)
- Move-in date availability
- Proof of lease (ask for a sample lease, not the offer letter—the actual lease document)

**Schedule a second video tour** in the evening or late afternoon. Noise is different. Lighting is different. You see the real vibe.

**Call current residents.** This is hard but possible:
- Ask the leasing office: "Can you put me in touch with a current resident who might talk about living here?"
- Some buildings have resident WhatsApp groups. Join and ask questions.
- Post on Miami-specific Reddit or Facebook groups asking for experiences in that building.

**Hire a local proxy.** If you're serious:
- Pay a friend $50–$100 to visit the building in person, take photos/videos, test things.
- Or hire a local agent (Don can do this) to do a walk-through and FaceTime you.

## Green Lights on Video

- Leasing agent is genuinely patient and thorough.
- Unit is bright, quiet, clean.
- HVAC turns on quietly and circulates air smoothly.
- Water pressure is strong.
- Walls look solid (no light gaps, no visible cracks).
- Building entrance is well-maintained.
- Common areas are clean and occupied (not empty).
- Neighborhood looks walkable and safe on video.

## The Reality

Video tours are 60% of the information. You're missing: wall feel, true noise levels, humidity (everything feels humid on Miami video, but some buildings are worse), genuine neighborhood vibe, and your gut reaction.

The best remote tour is still inferior to walking a building in person. If you can, fly down for 24 hours, tour 4–5 buildings, and sign within the week. If you can't, video tour + local proxy is next best.

A video tour can't replace walking a building yourself, but it can get you most of the way there if you know what to ask for on camera and what a screen genuinely can't tell you. Remote apartment hunting works better with a checklist than with a general sense of "it looked nice."

## Before the video call: have your questions ready

Ask the leasing agent to show you, specifically:

- The bedroom with the door closed, so you can see how much outside light comes through
- The HVAC running, so you can hear it
- The shower and sink running, to check water pressure
- A window opened, to check how much outside noise comes through
- Every closet, with the door open
- The parking garage, if applicable, including the walk from it to the elevators
- The hallway outside the unit, at the actual floor

Ask direct questions too: what do current residents raise most often with the leasing office, and what's the typical length of stay in the building. A leasing office that answers directly is more useful than one that only shows you the best angles.

## What a video call can't actually tell you about noise

A phone's microphone automatically suppresses background sound, and video compression flattens low-frequency noise like bass, HVAC rumble, or distant traffic — so a call that sounds quiet is not proof the room is quiet. Treat any noise impression from a video tour as a starting point, not a conclusion; see our [full noise-check guide](/blog/check-apartment-noise-before-signing) for how to verify it in person or through a live, unedited walkthrough instead of a produced video.

## Red flags to watch for on video

- Dim or visibly worn hallways
- Visible gaps around windows or doors
- HVAC that sounds like it's straining, even accounting for microphone limitations
- Weak water pressure when they run the tap
- A leasing agent who avoids a direct question or redirects instead of answering
- A pre-recorded or heavily edited video offered in place of a live call

## What a solid remote tour looks like

The agent shows you what you ask for, not just a pre-set path, on a live call rather than a produced clip. They answer direct questions without deflecting. Common areas look maintained rather than staged for a handful of camera angles.

## After the call: verify in writing

Follow up by email and request the floor plan, a complete list of required fees, the actual lease document (not just the offer letter), and move-in date availability. Ask if you can see a sample lease before applying, not after.

## Get a second set of eyes if you can

If it's a major decision, consider having someone you trust visit in person, or ask about hiring a local agent to do a walkthrough and call you live from the unit — that's the only way to get a genuine read on noise and feel, since no video call can fully substitute for standing in the room.

Remote touring gets you most of the information you need. The gap is always what you can't fully sense through a screen — treat a strong video tour as the start of your research, not the final word.
