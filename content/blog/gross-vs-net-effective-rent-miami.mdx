Two terms come up constantly when a Miami building is running a concession, and mixing them up leads to the wrong comparison.

## Gross rent is the sticker price

Gross rent is the advertised base rent before any concession — the number on the listing site or the leasing sign. It's real, but if the building is offering a free month or a dollar credit, it isn't what your average monthly cost works out to.

## Effective rent is the lease-term average, not your monthly bill

Effective rent is the total rent paid over the full lease, divided evenly across every month. It's a useful number for comparing two offers with different concessions — but it is an average, not a prediction of what any single statement will show. How a concession actually appears on your bill depends on the building: some zero out one specific month, some spread a smaller credit across several months, and some apply it as a credit at signing. Ask which method applies before you assume your rent will simply be "free" in one specific month on paper.

## A short example

A $2,500-a-month, 12-month lease with one month free: you'd pay rent in eleven of those months, for $27,500 total. Divide by 12 and the effective rent is $2,291.67 a month. That's hypothetical math to show the formula, not an available offer — and it still doesn't tell you whether your actual statement shows $2,500 in eleven months and $0 in one, or a smaller reduction spread across all twelve. Ask the leasing office directly for the payment schedule, not just the average.
