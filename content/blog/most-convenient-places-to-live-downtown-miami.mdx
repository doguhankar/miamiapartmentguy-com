## Build your five-trip test

List work, groceries, exercise, a frequent social destination and one necessary errand. Give each trip a frequency. A commute you make five days a week should count more than a restaurant you visit monthly. Check the route from the resident entrance, not the leasing office if they are different.

## For transit: include every part of the trip

The <a href="https://www.miamidade.gov/global/transportation/metromover.page">official Metromover page</a> describes service in Downtown, Omni and Brickell. Use its current map and service information for planning. Walk to the station, identify transfers and time the final leg to your destination. Check the station access you need instead of assuming a nearby marker means the whole trip is accessible or convenient.

## For driving: start inside the garage

Ask where your assigned or permitted space would be, how access works and what happens with visitors. Test the exit during your usual departure period where practical. Include the time from your front door to the street. Confirm the full parking charge, whether the space is reserved and whether a second vehicle changes the quote.

## For walking: carry the routine through

Walk to the grocery entrance and back. Check crossings, shade, your likely load and the building entry process. Consider whether you would still use that route in rain or heat. If deliveries are part of your plan, ask about package access and grocery drop-off. You are evaluating your own usable route, not assigning the whole neighborhood a walkability score.

## Keep a simple decision record

For each apartment, note the five trips, recurring cost, parking setup and any unresolved questions. If the location saves you time but the unit fails your sleep or layout needs, keep looking. The most convenient option is the apartment that handles your actual life across the whole week, not the one that wins a single commute test.
