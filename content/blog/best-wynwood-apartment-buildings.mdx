# The Best Types of Wynwood Apartment Buildings (And How to Spot Them)

**Wynwood's apartments come in three flavors: boutique conversions, newer luxury, and Miami Deco restored. Know the difference before you sign.**

Wynwood isn't generic. Its best apartments live in buildings that respect the neighborhood's DNA—converted warehouses, restored historic structures, and a handful of new builds that actually fit the vibe instead of looking like they landed from another city.

## Boutique Conversions and Restored Warehouses

These are the Instagram buildings. Old industrial warehouses turned into loft-style apartments with 12-foot ceilings, exposed brick, and giant windows. They're usually 100-200 units, owned by smaller developers who actually thought about the space. Hallways have character. Common areas feel intentional. Problem: older buildings, which means aging HVAC, plumbing quirks, and sound travels easily between units.

Rent runs $1,800–$2,500 for a one-bedroom. Leasing is usually flexible—month-to-month options, shorter terms. The vibe attracts artists, creatives, younger professionals who care about the neighborhood experience, not just the unit.

## Miami Deco Restored

Historic Art Deco buildings from the 1920s-1940s got restored in the last decade. These have charm that new buildings can't fake—curved corners, terrazzo lobbies, vintage hardware. They're usually smaller (50-100 units), so community feels tighter. Soundproofing is mid. Parking is usually street or lot-based, not underground. But the aesthetic is unmatched.

These rent between $1,600–$2,200. Leasing offices tend to be smaller, more personal. Ask about the HVAC system's age—older buildings retrofitted with new units are solid. Ones still running original 1940s systems? Skip it.

## Newer Luxury (The Outsiders)

Some new buildings landed in Wynwood in the last three years. Modern finishes, good soundproofing, rooftop bars. They're nice but sometimes feel like they belong in Brickell. They're usually $2,200+. Pros: quality construction, modern amenities, good insulation. Cons: higher rent, less neighborhood character, parking likely valet or expensive.

## The Real Move

Boutique and restored buildings in Wynwood are where the character is. Visit mid-week to see what the community actually feels like without the weekend party crowd. Ask longtime residents what they love and hate. Check if your unit is above a restaurant (sound carries up). Newer luxury works if character doesn't matter and you want reliability.
