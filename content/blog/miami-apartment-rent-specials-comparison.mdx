Buildings generally offer rent specials in one of two shapes: a number of free or discounted months, or a flat percentage off the whole lease term. They can look similar on a flyer and produce different real costs — and different lease-length commitments. The decision isn't just which number is bigger; it's whether the lease length attached to it fits your actual plans.

## Reduce both offers to the same number first

Whatever the special, use effective rent — the total you'd pay over the lease, divided by the number of months — to compare them fairly. See our [gross vs. effective rent guide](/blog/gross-vs-net-effective-rent-miami) for that definition, or the [months-free calculator](/blog/months-free-rent-miami-calculator) to run the full arithmetic including fees. The short version: a free-month offer and a percentage-off offer can land on very different effective rents even when the headline numbers look close.

## The real decision is the lease-length trade-off

A percentage-off special often comes attached to a longer lease than a free-month special. A lower effective rent on an 18-month lease only wins if you were planning to stay that long anyway. Before comparing the numbers, ask each building: "What lease length is this specific offer tied to?" If you're not sure how long you'll stay, also ask what it costs to break the lease early, and weigh that risk against the shorter option's slightly higher effective rent.

## Check whether fees are included in the special

Ask specifically whether parking, amenity, or other required fees are waived or reduced during the special, or whether they apply at full price from day one. A free month that doesn't touch your other monthly fees saves you less than it first appears to — and a credit that doesn't say how it will appear on your statement is worth confirming before you sign.

## What to ask the leasing office

- "What lease length is tied to this specific special, and is a shorter or longer term available at a different rate?"
- "What's the effective monthly rent for each option, including any required fees?"
- "What's the lease-break policy and cost if my plans change?"
- "Will this rate be available again at renewal, or does it reset to standard pricing?"

Whichever special looks better on the flyer, run it through the same effective-rent math as the other option, and weigh the lease length it's tied to, before deciding. The bigger discount isn't always the better deal once that's factored in.
