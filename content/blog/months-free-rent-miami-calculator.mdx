A Miami apartment offering “two months free” can lower your average rent over the lease. It does not automatically mean the monthly bill will be lower. To compare the deal, separate the stated monthly rent, the total concession, and the payment schedule in the written offer.

## The basic calculation
For a lease quoted in whole months, with free months credited at the same base monthly rent:

**Net effective base rent = (monthly base rent × lease months − rent credit) ÷ lease months.**

If the offer gives free months rather than a dollar credit, the rent credit is monthly base rent multiplied by the number of free months. This calculation describes an average over the full lease. It is not a promise about your monthly invoice.

For offers in weeks, partial months, or days, use the exact dollar credit from the written quote. Dividing weeks by four can misstate the concession because calendar months are not uniformly four weeks long.

## Example: two months free on a 14-month lease
Suppose the stated rent is $3,200 and the offer gives two full months of base rent free over a 14-month lease.

| Item | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Base rent before credit | $44,800 |
| Concession | $6,400 |
| Total base rent after credit | $38,400 |
| Net effective monthly base rent | $2,742.86 |

These are illustrative figures, not a live apartment offer. The property might charge the full $3,200 in paying months and apply credits in designated months. Ask whether spreading the credit across monthly payments is permitted; do not assume it is.

## Fees change the comparison
Parking, required service charges, and other recurring costs can change which apartment is cheaper. Keep optional choices separate from required charges so the comparison remains useful.

In the example above, adding an illustrative $150 in required monthly fees brings the average recurring cost to $2,892.86 before utilities or optional charges. A refundable deposit is a cash requirement, but it is not the same thing as a nonrefundable rental expense.

## Compare the same period
A longer lease can make a concession look larger while committing you to more months. Compare both the total obligation and the monthly average. If two offers have different lease lengths, also consider whether each term matches your actual plans.

A lower headline average can still require more cash during the first few months. Request a month-by-month payment schedule that includes the timing of credits, recurring charges, and move-in amounts.

## What the calculator should show
Enter monthly base rent, lease length, and either free months or a dollar credit. Add known required monthly fees and nonrefundable one-time fees separately. The results should show total base rent, total concession, net effective base rent, average recurring cost, and average cost including one-time fees.

The calculator should never label the average as “your monthly payment.” Your lease and concession agreement determine actual billing.

## Before you sign
Ask which units and move dates qualify, how the credit is applied, and whether eligibility depends on a specific lease length. Read any conditions concerning early termination or loss of the concession. If the documents are unclear, get an explanation before committing.
