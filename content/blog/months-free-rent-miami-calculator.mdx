"Two months free" can be a useful deal. It can also make two very different apartments look like the same price. Separate the base rent, concession, required fees and payment schedule before deciding what you can afford.

## Gross rent and net effective rent answer different questions

Monthly base rent is the stated rent before the concession. Multiply it by the lease length to get gross base rent over the term. Subtract the total credit, then divide by the lease months to get net effective monthly base rent. That is an average. It does not tell you which month gets a credit or the amount due on each monthly statement — some buildings zero out a specific month, others spread the credit as a smaller reduction across several months. Ask which one applies before you budget around it.

## Use the written offer, not the banner

Ask for the exact unit, lease dates, base rent, eligible credit and the way that credit is applied. Confirm any application or move-in deadline attached to the offer. Ask what happens to the concession if the lease ends early and review the written terms. Do not assume the credit applies to parking, utilities or every fee.

## A worked example

At $3,200 base rent for 14 months with two free months, gross base rent is $44,800 and the credit is $6,400. Net base rent over the term is $38,400, or $2,742.86 per month on average. Add $150 in required monthly fees and the recurring average becomes $2,892.86. These are hypothetical inputs, not an available apartment offer.

## Run your own numbers

Use either free months or a fixed dollar credit. For an offer quoted in weeks or days, get the actual dollar credit from the property rather than treating a month as four weeks. Include nonrefundable one-time fees separately. Refundable deposits belong in your cash-to-move budget.[[CALCULATOR]]

## Compare cost and cash flow

A lease can have an attractive average while still requiring the full base rent in most months. Ask for a payment schedule and make sure the ordinary bill fits your budget. Use the same lease length and included costs when comparing offers. Keep utility estimates, optional parking or pet costs in your own budget if they are not entered here, and do not count prepaid rent twice.
