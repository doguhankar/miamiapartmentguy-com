# Condo vs. Apartment: Which Rental Situation Is Actually Better in Miami?

**Condo rentals and apartment buildings are different games. Cheaper condo vs. easier apartment. Here's how to pick.**

In Miami, you're renting either a condo (owner-leased unit in a building where residents own individually) or an apartment (professionally managed building where the company owns everything). Both are legit. Both have hard trade-offs.

## Condos: Cheaper, Slower, More Uncertainty

Condo buildings are towers where individual owners buy units and rent them out. You're renting from a person managing their investment. Benefits: rent is $150–$400 cheaper than comparable managed buildings. Owners are often flexible on lease length (6 months, 14 months, whatever). Sometimes they care less about credit (though HOA rules apply). Drawbacks: owner approval takes 2–4 weeks. You'll submit your financials, background, ID. Some owners ghost or flake. Lease renewal isn't guaranteed—if the owner sells next year, you get a non-renewal notice.

Typical condo in Brickell: $2,000/month. First month, last month, deposit ($6,000 day-one). Approval: 3 weeks.

## Apartments: More Expensive, Instant, Reliable

Professionally managed buildings (Cortland, The Preiss, Equity Residential properties, etc.) own everything. Benefits: approval is 3–5 days. You pass their screening or you don't—no owner discretion. Lease is professional—terms are clear, renewal is predictable. You can extend next year. Amenities are usually better. Problem: rent is higher. Same unit in a managed building runs $2,400–$2,600. Move-in is faster but costs hit harder (amenity fees, parking fees, concierge fees).

Typical apartment in Brickell: $2,400/month. Amenity fee: $250/month. Parking: $200/month. Total annual: $31,800. Day-one: $7,200 (first, last, deposit).

## The Real Trade-Off

Condos: You save $150–$300/month rent, but you wait longer, approval is uncertain, and lease renewal isn't guaranteed. Best if you're stable, have solid credit, and plan to stay 1–2 years.

Apartments: You pay more, but you get certainty, fast approval, and predictable renewal. Best if you're corporate, new to Miami, or moving frequently.

## The Math

**Condo for 18 months**: $36,000 rent + $6,000 move-in = $42,000. Savings vs. apartment: $2,700–$5,400.

**Apartment for 18 months**: $43,200 rent + (let's say) $250/month amenity fee + $200/month parking = $51,300 move-in. Cost: $8,700 move-in = $60,000 total.

Condos win financially. Apartments win logistically.
